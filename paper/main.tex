\begin{abstract}
We report an empirical, key-free indicator for the presence of Google
DeepMind's public SynthID-Text instance (\texttt{public-deepmind-30}).
The auditor is a \emph{paired marked/unmarked} observer: it holds matched
twins from the same prompts, not detector keys, hash initialization
vectors, or $g$-values. That is a strong oracle. It is not detection of
an arbitrary web document.

The result is a two-grain measurement, not a universal detector.
At the \textbf{population grain} (prompt-group ranking of mean empirical
log-likelihood ratios), held-out groups rank marked above unmarked
\textbf{9/12} times on the original 12-prompt leave-one-out hard last-4
tables (\textbf{10/12} with a $0.02$ comparison margin),
\textbf{36/36} times for in-domain hits on 36 topics $\times$ 4 draws
(file AUC $0.930$), and \textbf{99/100} times on a confirmatory lock of
100 new GPT-2 families. Cross-generator opening tables on those same 100
prompts rank \textbf{88/100} on DistilGPT-2 (one tie) and
\textbf{95/100} on Qwen2-1.5B. A ranking win can occur when both group
means are negative, if the unmarked mean is more negative.

At the \textbf{isolated grain} (one 128-token file, sign of the same
ratio against zero), hard last-4 on the original 12 is
\textbf{25/48} (52.1\%; Clopper--Pearson 95\% interval
$[0.372, 0.667]$, which includes $1/2$). Isolated classification is
chance-like. Occupancy leftover files on that 12 are
\textbf{10/20} vs.\ \textbf{11/20} under the same hard reader, while
the official keyed detector still marks those leftover files
\textbf{20/20}. Key-free argmax snap on the 48 marked 12$\times$4 files
moves the official mean from $0.622$ to $0.499$.

The indicator is key-free, not reference-free, and not fully blind.
It does not recover keys. It does not refute the cryptographic
undetectability construction of \citet{christ2024undetectable} or the
strong-watermarking impossibility of \citet{zhang2024sand}. Isolated-file
research on this mixin is not finished.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
statistical text watermarking, SynthID-Text, key-free indication,
empirical likelihood ratio, paired-reference auditing.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Statistical watermarks for language models bias next-token sampling with
a secret keyed function, then test whether a finished string contains
more of that structure than chance \citep{kirchenbauer2023watermark,
aaronson2023watermark,dathathri2024synthid}. Google DeepMind's
SynthID-Text is a tournament member of that family, deployed at scale
and released as a public reference implementation
\citep{dathathri2024synthid,deepmind2024synthidcode}. Official detection
needs the instance keys. If the keys are withheld, third-party
verification is an open practical question
\citep{deepmind2024issue22,gloaguen2025blackbox,wang2026ttp}.

This laboratory asks a scoped version of that question:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Given matched marked and unmarked generations from the same
prompts, and no detector keys, can an auditor tell whether this public
tournament instance is on---as a population ranking of prompt groups,
and as a classifier of one 128-token file?}
\end{quote}

The two grains must not be collapsed. Prompt-group ranking without keys
is real on the measured corpora. Isolated 128-token classification at
threshold zero is not a calibrated detector. A slogan of the form
``key-free detection works'' or ``key-free detection fails'' is false
for this notebook.

\subsection{What is new here}

Independent work already asked whether a third party can detect a
sampling watermark without the vendor's keys
(\Cref{sec:related}). \citet{gloaguen2025blackbox} test a
\emph{generator} property with black-box queries.
\citet{wang2026ttp} verify finished strings from paired
watermarked/unwatermarked references---the closest published analog of
the present audit. This repository is a small, fully checked-in instance
of a cheaper method on DeepMind's public mixin: count-table likelihood
ratios, leave-one-prompt-out, frozen confirmatory locks, occupancy-free
readouts, and an honest isolated-file miss.

The contribution, in order, is:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item A cheap empirical log-likelihood ratio on matched twins, with no
keys, \texttt{hash\_iv}, or $g$-values at decision time.
\item A two-grain measurement on \texttt{public-deepmind-30}:
population ranking \textbf{9/12}, \textbf{36/36}, lock~A
\textbf{99/100}; isolated hard sign \textbf{25/48}.
\item A confirmatory 100-family freeze (lock~A interpolate last-4) and a
cross-generator check that DistilGPT-2's weaker official lamp
(\textbf{70/100}) is watermark strength, not only indicator failure.
\item Ablations that remove two artifacts---truncated-context overcount
and Laplace occupancy---without erasing prompt-group ranking, and a
key-free argmax snap that returns official scores to chance.
\end{enumerate}

What this is \emph{not}: key recovery; a web-crawler detector; a
calibrated isolated-file classifier; a production Gemini or Claude
measurement; a complexity-theoretic distinguisher against
\citet{christ2024undetectable}; a mixing attack against
\citet{zhang2024sand}.

\subsection{Locked headlines}

Unless a number below is labelled as an ablation, it is a locked
headline of this repository after a truncated-context recount
(\Cref{sec:overcount}):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Official keyed \texttt{detector\_mean} on the original 12 twins:
\textbf{12/12}.
\item Key-free hard last-4, 12-prompt leave-one-out: ranking
\textbf{9/12} (Clopper--Pearson 95\% $[0.428, 0.945]$, includes $1/2$);
margin $0.02$ gives \textbf{10/12} ($[0.516, 0.979]$, excludes $1/2$).
\item Isolated hard sign, same tables: marked $\mathrm{lr}>0$ is
\textbf{25/48} ($[0.372, 0.667]$); unmarked $\mathrm{lr}\le 0$ is
\textbf{22/48}. File AUC $0.590$; file-level permutation $p=0.040$
(descriptive). Binomial $P(\ge 25 \mid n=48, p=1/2)\approx 0.44$.
\item In-domain hits, 36 topics $\times$ 4 draws: \textbf{36/36} prompt
wins, file AUC $0.930$, permutation $p\approx 5\times 10^{-4}$.
\item Confirmatory GPT-2 100$\times$4 lock~A (interpolate last-4):
\textbf{99/100}. DistilGPT-2 lock~B \textbf{88/100} (1~tie); Qwen2-1.5B
lock~B \textbf{95/100}. Isolated \textbf{25/48} is not replaced.
\item Occupancy leftover of those 25 true positives:
\textbf{10/20} vs.\ \textbf{11/20}; official keyed leftover
\textbf{20/20}.
\item Argmax snap on 48 marked files: official mean
$\mathbf{0.622 \to 0.499}$.
\end{itemize}

and stay in historical JSON.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

This laboratory did not invent watermarking, key-free detection, or
watermark stealing.

\subsection{Sampling watermarks and keyed detection}

\citet{kirchenbauer2023watermark} bias sampling with a keyed green list
and a $z$-test. \citet{aaronson2023watermark} describe a Gumbel /
exponential-minimum scheme (unpublished talk, cited from
\citealt{dathathri2024synthid}). \citet{kuditipudi2024robust} give a
distortion-free inverse-transform construction with alignment detection.
SynthID-Text is a tournament variant: keyed $g$-values reweight the
language model's top-$k$, and official detectors average those $g$-values
\citep{dathathri2024synthid}. This repository's \texttt{score} path is
the public reference implementation \citep{deepmind2024synthidcode}. We
do not reimplement \texttt{detector\_mean}.

\citet{omidi2026synthid} analyse SynthID-Text's \emph{keyed} scores and
a layer-inflation removal attack. That object is official $g$-value
detection with keys. It is not the present indicator, and this laboratory
does not implement that attack. \citet{han2025synguard} stress SynthID
with paraphrase, copy-paste, and back-translation. The current workflow
does not download DIPPER \citep{krishna2023dipper}.

\subsection{Undetectability and strong watermarking}

\citet{christ2024undetectable} prove cryptographic undetectability for a
stated PRF-based construction: a polynomial-time algorithm without the
key cannot distinguish watermarked from unwatermarked text with
non-negligible advantage, even with adaptive queries. A refutation would
be a proof error or an efficient distinguisher against \emph{that}
construction. This laboratory did not instantiate that scheme.

\citet{zhang2024sand} prove impossibility of \emph{strong} watermarking
given a quality oracle and a mixing perturbation oracle. A refutation
would be a scheme that remains detectable after those oracles, or a hole
in that argument. This laboratory did not run those oracles. Official
keyed leftover detection uses keys and is not a mixing attack.

Those theorems are not a proof that count tables must fail on
\texttt{public-deepmind-30}, and lock~A \textbf{99/100} is not a
complexity-theoretic distinguisher against their schemes. Isolated
\textbf{25/48} is a different grain, not a restoration of those theorems.

\subsection{Stealing, public verifiability, third-party detection}

\citet{jovanovic2024stealing} learn a spoof/remove model from black-box
API samples. Concurrent stealing and spoofing include
\citet{wu2024bypassing} and \citet{pang2024nfl}. We do not steal keys,
train a spoof generator, or invert \texttt{hash\_iv} from a static
string.

\citet{duan2025pvmark} wrap keyed detectors in a zero-knowledge proof so
a third party can check that detection ran faithfully. The detector still
uses the key. That is not this indicator. Google DeepMind's public
feature request for publicly verifiable detection remains open
\citep{deepmind2024issue22}.

\citet{gloaguen2025blackbox} detect a watermarking \emph{scheme as a
generator property} with limited black-box queries. SRI Lab
\citep{gloaguen2024probingsynthid} applied those tests to a local
SynthID-Text deployment. That is not scoring a finished string against
count tables.

\citet{wang2026ttp} (TTP-Detect) formulate third-party, key-agnostic
verification from observable outputs and paired
watermarked/unwatermarked reference sets. Same audit problem; different
method (proxy model and relative tests, not count-table LR). This
notebook is in that second family. It is not a claim to have surpassed
Wang et al.\ on a shared benchmark, and it does not reimplement
TTP-Detect.

Anthropic's announced Claude mark remains a future external test
\citep{anthropic2026watermarknews,anthropic2026watermarkhelp}. Production
keys are not public. Official \texttt{score} on
\texttt{public-deepmind-30} cannot read that instance.

\section{Preliminaries and Threat Model}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{SynthID-Text on the public instance}

Let $\V$ be the generator vocabulary. An autoregressive model supplies
$P_0(x_t \mid x_{<t})$. SynthID-Text does not insert hidden characters.
It biases which token is sampled \citep{dathathri2024synthid}.

The public reference configuration used here is
\texttt{public-deepmind-30}: $\mathtt{ngram\_len}=5$ (context length
$H=4$), 30 public keys, $\mathtt{context\_history\_size}=1024$. The
implementation hashes the recent context with a linear congruential
generator. Trust the code for $g$-values: 12 LCG mixes, then
$(\mathrm{hash} \gg 30) \bmod 2$. The mixin's docstring (``lowest three
bits'') does not match that implementation. $g$-values are binary, not
continuous scores in $[0,1]$.

Only the language model's top-$k$ candidates are reweighted
($k=40$ in this laboratory; temperature $1.0$). Repeated context hashes
are masked so they do not add repeated evidence. The first $H$ generated
tokens cannot form a complete scored context.

The official mean detector averages unmasked $g$-values of observed
tokens. Under unmarked text, $\E[g]=1/2$. With the matching keys,
tournament winners push the mean above $1/2$ (empirically about $0.62$ on
the original 12 marked twins; \Cref{tab:official_12}). Distortion-freeness
in expectation over random keys is a claim of the scheme
\citep{dathathri2024synthid}, not a theorem proved in this notebook. A
deployed instance fixes one key set.

\subsection{The paired auditor}

\begin{definition}[Key-free paired auditor]
\label{def:auditor}
The auditor $\mathcal{A}$ has black-box access to two configurations of
the same generator and tokenizer:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item unmarked $G_0$, producing $x^{(u)}\sim P_0(\cdot \mid p)$;
\item marked $G_w$, producing $x^{(m)}\sim P_w(\cdot \mid p)$ under a
fixed public instance.
\end{enumerate}
It holds a paired training corpus of finished strings
\[
\D_{\mathrm{train}}
= \bigl\{(p_i, x_{i,j}^{(m)}, x_{i,j}^{(u)})\bigr\}
\]
across $N$ prompt families and $M$ draws per family. It does
\emph{not} have the instance keys, \texttt{hash\_iv}, $g$-value tables,
or the official scoring oracle at decision time. Official
\texttt{score} is a positive control that the mixin is on, not the
contribution.
\end{definition}

This is a strong oracle. A reviewer who writes ``you needed marked and
unmarked training pairs'' is describing the threat model correctly. It
is a legitimate audit setting (before/after, or a lab that can toggle
the mixin). It is not ``one stranger's file, no corpus, no keys.''
The method is \textbf{key-free}. It is \textbf{not reference-free} and
\textbf{not fully blind}. In this repository, \texttt{blind} means
leave-one-prompt-out on those twins.

\subsection{Two evaluation grains}

Let $\LLR(x)$ be the empirical mean log-likelihood ratio of
\Cref{sec:method}.

\paragraph{Task 1: prompt-group ranking (population grain).}
For a held-out prompt $p$ with $M$ marked and $M$ unmarked draws, declare
a win if
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M} \LLR\bigl(x_{p,j}^{(m)}\bigr)
>
\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M} \LLR\bigl(x_{p,j}^{(u)}\bigr).
\label{eq:ranking}
\end{equation}
Ties are not awarded. Both means may be negative: a win then means the
unmarked group is \emph{more negative}, not that any marked file is
positive.

\paragraph{Task 2: isolated-file sign (single-text grain).}
For one file $x$ of $T=128$ generated tokens,
\begin{equation}
\hat y(x) = \mathbf{1}\{\LLR(x)>0\}.
\label{eq:isolated}
\end{equation}
No twin is shown at test time. Threshold $0$ is not a Youden threshold
fit on the same stem.

The independent unit for ranking inference is the prompt family.
Leave-one-prompt-out tables share training mass, so families are weakly
dependent. File-level permutation and binomial $p$-values are
descriptive \citep{clopper1934binomial}. Exact McNemar tests on
discordant prompt-family signs are clustered descriptions of
already-opened scores, not a second freeze \citep{mcnemar1947sampling}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Paired generation}

All twins use Hugging Face generators with the public mixin
(\texttt{min\_new\_tokens}=\texttt{max\_new\_tokens}=$128$, temperature
$1.0$, top-$k=40$). Marked and unmarked files for a prompt share the
prompt string and differ by whether the mixin is on. Control-key files,
when used, are a laboratory shuffle of the public 30
(\texttt{control-shuffled-30}), not a second vendor.

Corpora used in the locked tables:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Original 12 Grok-length scene prompts, GPT-2, 4 draws
(12$\times$4). Headline 12-LOO.
\item 36 in-domain GPT-2 topics $\times$ 4 draws (36$\times$4).
\item 100 new English one-line scene prompts, disjoint as strings from
the 36, seed \texttt{20260901} (100$\times$4). Confirmatory lock.
\item The same 100 prompts on DistilGPT-2 and Qwen2-1.5B-Instruct
(Phase~B). Lock~A interpolate was not run on those twins.
\end{itemize}

GPT-2 Medium occupancy-free analogs exist and are reported as such
(\Cref{sec:medium}). Prompt-group locks A/B/C were not run on Medium.

\subsection{Empirical mean log-likelihood ratio}

Fit two next-token count tables on the training twins, context length
$k=4$ unless noted. Generated token $0$ is skipped by default (no
complete last-4 context without the prompt). Smoothing is Lidstone with
$\alpha=0.5$, not add-one Laplace. For a context $c$ and token $y$,
\begin{equation}
\hat P(y \mid c)
= \frac{C(c,y)+\alpha}{\sum_{y'} C(c,y') + \alpha V},
\label{eq:lidstone}
\end{equation}
falling back to the unigram when $c$ is unseen (\texttt{hard}). The
file score is the \emph{mean} per scored position, not a sum:
\begin{equation}
\LLR(x)
= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{t \in \mathcal{I}(x)}
\log \frac{\Phatm(x_t \mid c_t)}{\Phatu(x_t \mid c_t)},
\label{eq:llr}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{I}(x)$ are scored generated indices and $n=|\mathcal{I}(x)|$.
Hats are essential: the auditor never observes the true $P_w$.

\paragraph{Readers.}
\texttt{hard} uses \eqref{eq:lidstone} with unigram fallback.
\texttt{hits} scores only last-4 grams observed in both training piles.
\texttt{poshits} namespaces counts by position bucket.
\texttt{postokhits} scores a position only if the next token was observed
under that context (occupancy-free; else the position abstains).
\texttt{interpolate} uses Witten--Bell backoff through shorter contexts.
\texttt{rankpath} replaces token identity with a five-symbol unmarked-LM
rank (miss top-$k$, or rank $1$--$4$ inside top-$k$). Hash pooling in
this repository is a laboratory splitmix of token ids. It is not
SynthID's secret hash and not the stealing attack of
\citet{jovanovic2024stealing}.

\paragraph{Occupancy.}
If a context is seen in training but the evaluation next token is not,
add-$\alpha$ still assigns a nonzero log ratio from the two occupancy
denominators. That occupancy mass is not an observed-token hit.
\texttt{postokhits} removes it. Witten--Bell interpolate can still score
unseen next tokens by backoff; nested interpolate recall is therefore
not occupancy-free recall.

\subsection{Validation}

Leave-one-prompt-out: tables for family $p$ are fit on all other
families. Nested Youden-by-stem, when reported, chooses a threshold on
the other families' already-held-out scores. That is a threshold nest,
not second-level nested cross-validation. Primary isolated headline
uses $t=0$, not Youden. Prompt ranking uses strict $>$
(\Cref{eq:ranking}).

\subsection{Confirmatory locks and hypotheses}
\label{sec:hypotheses}

Before generating the 100 new GPT-2 families, three readers were named:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Lock A.} Interpolate last-4 (\texttt{--methods interpolate
--context-len 4}).
\item \textbf{Lock B.} Opening poshits (\texttt{--methods poshits
--fit-prefix 4 --pos-bucket 1}).
\item \textbf{Lock C.} Opening rankpath (\texttt{--rankpath
--fit-prefix 4 --pos-bucket 1}).
\end{itemize}

\begin{hypothesis}[H1]
\label{hyp:h1}
On new GPT-2 prompt families, lock~A prompt ranking
\eqref{eq:ranking} exceeds chance (primary confirmatory endpoint).
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{hypothesis}[H2]
\label{hyp:h2}
Under lock~A, window $0{:}4$ ranks more families than window
$16{:}32$ (opening sufficient in-domain).
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{hypothesis}[H3]
\label{hyp:h3}
Lock~C (rankpath) is more generator-specific than lock~B (opening
poshits): on DistilGPT-2 and Qwen2-1.5B, rankpath prompt-win drop from
GPT-2 Phase~A exceeds the poshits drop.
\end{hypothesis}

H1--H3 are predictions about prompt ranking. Isolated $\mathrm{lr}>0$
is logged and is not H1. Phase~B does not run lock~A.

\section{Why a Fixed Key Can Leave a Trace}
\label{sec:math}

Why should unkeyed counts separate twins if the scheme is
distortion-free in expectation over keys? The following is a sketch,
not a tight theorem. Empirical $\LLR$ uses $\Phatm$ and $\Phatu$, not
$P_w$.

A random-key expectation $\E_g[P_w(\cdot \mid c)]=P_0(\cdot \mid c)$
does not imply that one fixed instance is identical to $P_0$. Tournament
sampling on a fixed $g$-table reweights top-$k$ in a context-dependent
way. Repeated draws from the same prompt therefore reuse short contexts
($H=4$ on this mixin), and empirical next-token frequencies can differ
between marked and unmarked piles even though no key is recovered.

Let $\Delta_p$ be the prompt contrast \eqref{eq:ranking}. If the $2M$
draws are independent and per-draw $\LLR$ has finite variance, then
$\mathrm{Var}(\Delta_p)$ scales as $O(1/M)$. That is the population
grain: $M=4$ already reduces noise relative to one file. We do not
claim an exponential Hoeffding bound, and we do not claim a
signal-to-noise formula that would make isolated $T=128$ classification
impossible in general.

On one held-out 128-token string, most last-4 contexts are unseen under
leave-one-prompt-out. \texttt{hard} then falls back to a unigram;
\texttt{hits} and \texttt{postokhits} abstain. Isolated sign at $0$ then
tracks a few overlapping openings, occupancy artifacts, or backoff, not
a recovered payload. That is an empirical bound on this mixin and these
tables, not a proof that every key-free distinguisher fails.

Christ et al.\ and Zhang et al.\ apply to different constructions and
oracles (\Cref{sec:related}). Isolated \textbf{25/48} is compatible with
a weak single-file test. Population ranking of matched twins is outside
their single-challenge games as stated. Neither sentence is a theorem
about tournament sampling in their sense.

\section{Ablations, Artifacts, and Scrubbing}
\label{sec:ablations}

\subsection{Truncated-context overcount}
\label{sec:overcount}

Early logs stored every order $1..k$ even when fewer tokens existed, so
\texttt{fit\_table([[10,20]], context\_len=4)} counted $(10,)\to 20$
four times. After storing each real suffix once, hard last-4 ranking
moved from 10/12 to \textbf{9/12} and isolated sign from 29/48 to
\textbf{25/48}. Prompt ranking survived. Hits on the recounted twins is
\textbf{10/12} (AUC $0.718$). In-domain 36$\times$4 hits stayed
\textbf{36/36}. Pre-fix JSON is historical.

\subsection{Occupancy versus observed tokens}

On the 4-token new-topic gate, poshits isolated $t=0$ is 39/48 vs.\
41/48. Occupancy-free \texttt{postokhits} on that split is 16/48, with
decided precision 1.000: The-Laplace true positives become zeros.
36$\times$4 LOO poshits $t=0$ is 131/144 vs.\ 132/144; postokhits is
122/144 vs.\ 132/144 (nine of the poshits TPs were occupancy). Isolated
observed-token recall on named opening readers equals train opening-atom
overlap. That identity does not create a denser isolated-file detector.

\subsection{H2 windows (reindexed vs.\ absolute)}
\label{sec:h2}

H2 asked whether lock~A window $0{:}4$ outranks $16{:}32$ on the 100
GPT-2 families. The committed first dump scored each window as a
\emph{reindexed} substring: $0{:}4$ \textbf{99/100} (AUC $0.885$),
$16{:}32$ \textbf{89/100} (AUC $0.689$). An absolute-history remasure
that keeps preceding tokens as context gives $0{:}4$ \textbf{99/100}
(AUC $0.885$) and $16{:}32$ \textbf{87/100} (AUC $0.695$). Opening still
outranks mid-file in-family. Absolute 16:32 did not rise versus
reindexed 89/100. The reindexed dump is not overwritten.

Post-open paired prompt-family McNemar on the absolute windows is
$86/13/1/0$ (both / 0:4 only / 16:32 only / neither). One-sided
$P(X\ge 13 \mid \mathrm{Bin}(14,1/2))\approx 0.00092$
\citep{mcnemar1947sampling}. That is not a second freeze. Unpaired
interval overlap is not the H2 test. Isolated \textbf{25/48} is a
different grain.

Out-of-family, the same interpolate tables are \emph{not} front-loaded
on Grok-length files (reindexed $0{:}4$ is 7/12; tails 32:64 and 64:128
are 9/12). Absolute-history remasure: grok12 $0{:}4$ stays 7/12; 32:64
rises to 10/12 versus reindexed 9/12; 64:128 stays 9/12. Original 12
absolute $0{:}4$ is 9/12 vs.\ $16{:}32$ 6/12. Absolute 10/12 is not
sold as a headline.

\subsection{Mask-$k$ of the headline hard scorer}

Masking the first scored tokens of 12-LOO hard last-4 does not destroy
prompt ranking: tails $4{:}128$ and $8{:}128$ stay \textbf{9/12}, while
prefix $0{:}4$ is only \textbf{5/12}. Interpolate tails drop to 5/12
then 3/12. Headline hard \textbf{9/12} is not an opening-only $n$-gram
artifact. Isolated $t=0$ stays chance-like in every slice. Hard $4{:}128$
leftover is 11/20 vs.\ 11/20; covered 16/28. Tail 9/12 is
occupancy-covered ranking, not leftover-file recall.

\subsection{Control-key instance contrast}

Text sampled with \texttt{control-shuffled-30} scores near $0.50$ on the
public keys (mean of 48 file means $0.501$) and near $0.62$ on the
matching control keys ($0.624$). Key-free opening poshits trained on
public twins assign $\mathrm{lr}>0$ to \textbf{0/48} control files.
Public vs.\ control still ranks \textbf{12/12} (AUC $0.906$). The
count-table reader is instance-specific without recovering keys.
Control-vs-unmarked AUC $0.510$ is the honest ranking; prompt wins
against negative unmarked LRs are not evidence that control looks
marked.

A later in-domain lock~A with control-as-marked 12$\times$4 versus the
original unmarked pile ranks 7/12 (isolated 30/48 vs.\ 25/48). That
protocol is not a matched \texttt{pair()} run (seeds 20260931 vs.\ 0),
and the 30 isolated positives are control-key files, not the original
public marked 12. The count 30/48 is not sold as replacing
\textbf{25/48}.

\subsection{Argmax-snap scrubbing}
\label{sec:scrub}

Tournament sampling only reweights the unmarked top-$k$. Snapping each
generated token to the unmarked argmax, using original prefixes, is a
key-free removal attempt. It needs the unmarked language model, not the
keys. It is a statistical scrub, not a fluent rewrite.

On all 48 marked 12$\times$4 files, official mean moves
$\mathbf{0.6216 \to 0.4994}$. An unmarked control stays near half
($0.508 \to 0.487$). About 60--90 of 128 tokens flip. Binary
\texttt{snapupset} (in-top-$k$ but not argmax) is chance as a
\emph{detector} (opening 7/12, AUC $0.501$, marked/unmarked upset rates
$0.649$ vs.\ $0.653$). Scrub still kills the official mean because it
changes \emph{which} tokens occupy those non-argmax slots, and the
public detector reads $g$-values on the chosen ids.

\subsection{GPT-2 Medium occupancy-free analog}
\label{sec:medium}

GPT-2 Medium (355M, same BPE) was generated in the laboratory.
Prompt-group locks A/B/C were not run (Phase~B refused lock~A on Distil;
the Medium freeze stayed occupancy-free). Official first-draw lamp on
Medium 100$\times$4 twins is \textbf{100/100}. Occupancy-free leftover-15
coverage on the original GPT-2 12 is \textbf{0/15}; Medium$\to$GPT-2
\texttt{postokhits} $t=0$ is 16/48 vs.\ 48/48. Medium$\to$Medium 12
occupancy-free $t=0$ is 10/48 vs.\ 48/48 (coverage 13/48).
Distil$\leftrightarrow$Medium occupancy-free $t=0$ is 20/48 vs.\ 48/48
and 3/48 vs.\ 47/48. None of those counts replaces \textbf{25/48}.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\texttt{public-deepmind-30} is a short-context ($H=4$) public demo, not a
proprietary production key. Population ranking needs matched draws per
prompt. Isolated classification of one 128-token file at $t=0$ is
chance-like; this paper does not claim a key-free single-file detector.

The auditor is strong: marked and unmarked twins, finished strings, a
toggleable mixin. That is not web-text detection. Closest published
finished-string analog is \citet{wang2026ttp}; this repository does not
reimplement TTP-Detect on a shared new corpus.

DistilGPT-2 official \textbf{70/100} means cross-architecture numbers
are not pure indicator tests. Rankpath is generator-specific (H3).
Learned GPT-2 scorers do not transfer to Distil or Qwen.

Leftover \textbf{10/20} vs.\ \textbf{11/20} and official leftover
\textbf{20/20} are ablations of the isolated miss. They do not replace
\textbf{25/48}. Occupancy-free opening overlap (\texttt{Closing},
\texttt{The car}) is not a recovered SynthID payload.

The $\psi$-style tournament formula sometimes written for order
statistics is not a line-by-line match to DeepMind's implementation;
this paper does not use it as a proof. Empirical $\LLR$ uses hats.
Sketches in \Cref{sec:math} are not tight concentration theorems.

Production Gemini and Anthropic keys were not measured. DIPPER paraphrase
was not run. Multi-key replication of the public mixin is not started.
Isolated-file research is not finished.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On Google DeepMind's public SynthID-Text instance, a paired
marked/unmarked auditor can rank held-out prompt groups without detector
keys (\textbf{9/12}, \textbf{36/36}, confirmatory \textbf{99/100}). The
same tables do not classify one 128-token file at threshold zero
(\textbf{25/48}; 95\% interval includes $1/2$). Ranking wins can be
``unmarked more negative.'' Leftover isolated files remain officially
marked (\textbf{20/20}) while key-free last-4 on that slice is chance
(\textbf{10/20} vs.\ \textbf{11/20}). Argmax snap returns official
scores from about $0.62$ to $0.50$ without keys.

That is a two-grain empirical indication on a public mixin, under a
strong oracle. It is not a universal detector, not key recovery, and not
a refutation of \citet{christ2024undetectable} or \citet{zhang2024sand}.
Code and frozen JSON:
\texttt{https://github.com/jensabrahamsson/text-watermark-laboratory}.
