Thought for 1s

The gate has a padlock with a code, not a key, and someone has written the code in marker on the inside of the shed door where it's mostly worn off. Bean poles still stand in a wigwam shape though the beans finished three weeks ago, leaves gone yellow and papery, a few dried pods left rattling if you shake the frame. Someone left a trowel stuck upright in the soil by the second bed, handle silvered from rain, and it's been there long enough that a spider has strung a line from it to the nearest cabbage.

The cabbages are the main thing left standing. Big grey-green heads, some split from the wet, slug damage in dark tunnels through the outer leaves. Brussels sprouts on their stalks look wrong, almost architectural, like something assembled rather than grown. Leeks in a row, soil mounded up around the white part, a few gone to seed with heads like fists on sticks. The squash vines have collapsed into a brown tangle at the plot's edge, but three pumpkins sit in the middle of it, orange against the wreckage, one with a flat rotted side facing down into the dirt.

Someone's tomato plants are pulled and stacked against the fence in a pile that's started to compost itself, black at the bottom. The bamboo stakes are still in the ground, tomato clips still on them, nothing left to hold up. A green tomato sits on a windowsill of the little shed, put there weeks ago probably, waiting for red that isn't coming now. Netting sags over the raspberry canes, torn in one corner, and there are no raspberries in it, just wet leaves and a blackbird that doesn't leave when you walk past, just hops to the next row.

The paths between plots are mud in the low spots, gravel worn thin, a wheelbarrow left on its side by someone's compost bin with a puddle collected in its base. Compost bins themselves are the productive part of the whole allotment right now — steam actually rising off one in the cold morning, which surprises you the first time you see it. Dahlias are still going in the plot two down, defiantly red and orange, someone clearly dead-heading them into November if they can. Next to them, a stand of sunflowers gone to seed, heads bowed, husks empty where birds have already been at them.

A hand-painted sign nailed to a stick says PLEASE DO NOT PICK, faded, the plot behind it half-cleared, half a jungle of bolted lettuce and something with white flowers nobody planted on purpose. Frost hasn't come yet but the air has that flat, mineral smell that comes before it. A radio is playing faintly from somewhere near the far hedge, tinny, a voice more than music, and no one visible.
