The parking garage smelled like it always did at six in the morning, concrete and exhaust and the particular staleness of air that had been breathed by too many people going in too many directions. Elena took the stairs instead of the elevator because her legs needed something to do besides stand still, twelve hours of standing still in a different sense, the kind where you don't move your feet but your whole body is running.

Outside the light was doing that thing where it wasn't quite light yet, more like the dark thinning out from the edges. She'd stopped noticing sunrises years ago, or thought she had, but this one she noticed, maybe because Mr. Halvorsen in 412 had finally stopped fighting the ventilator around four, and she'd stood there for a minute after the family came in, not doing anything nursing-related, just standing.

She lived eleven blocks from the hospital and always walked it no matter the weather, because the bus at that hour was full of people who'd been up all night for reasons she didn't want to think too hard about, and because walking let the shift drain out of her by the time she got home, most of it anyway. Some of it stayed no matter how far she walked.

The street was quiet in the particular way streets are quiet before a city decides to start. A man was hosing down the sidewalk in front of a bakery, and the smell of bread underneath the wet pavement smell made her stomach remember it hadn't eaten since midnight. She didn't stop. If she stopped for bread she'd sit down somewhere and not want to get up, and then Marcus would have to come collect her, and he had his own shift to get to by eight.

Her phone buzzed twice, her sister probably, or the group chat about their mother's hip surgery, but she didn't look at it. There was a rule she'd made for herself a long time ago, no phone until the shower, because whatever was on it could wait the twenty minutes it took to get clean and human again.

A woman jogged past with a dog that had a little vest on it, and Elena watched them go with the specific tiredness that makes ordinary things briefly fascinating, the vest, the dog's tongue, the woman's breath making small clouds even though it wasn't that cold. Behind her the hospital was getting smaller, which always felt like an achievement, some nights more than others.

At her building she took the stairs again, second floor, and let herself in as quietly as she could, though Marcus was already up, she could hear the coffee maker doing its last gurgle. He didn't ask how it was. He'd learned not to, mostly, or he'd learned to wait until she offered something, which she usually did, later, after sleep, in pieces.

She set her shoes by the door, the ones with the good soles that were starting to wear thin at the heel, and stood for a second in the kitchen doorway before going to shower, watching him pour two cups instead of one.
