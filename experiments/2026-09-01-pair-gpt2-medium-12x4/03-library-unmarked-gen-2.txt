The last library I came to is an old school in a street that the school now calls the 'old street'. It opened in 1684 and was built by merchants that once ran the place. It is now the library for the town of Bristol which was founded at the end of a year by a wealthy merchant from Paris, Louis XIV. It is now part of the National Museum of Fine Arts in London. One of today's best-known collections is a portrait of the new king commissioned by the schoolmaster, William Henry Gartland, who commissioned two paintings of the king for the collection.
