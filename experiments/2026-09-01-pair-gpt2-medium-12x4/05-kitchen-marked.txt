Danger, I realized, lurks behind these two. My Aunt Jean never makes the pan the right way.

I turned my head toward Mr. Pinto's window and looked in his direction. "I had two little sores," he said in the dimness. "This is too hot." Mr. Pinto pulled a tin of white bread from under his elbow. His hand burned, though the smell of salt and vinegar never passed me as I stood in the gloom, the smoke of the cigarette in my mouth. He looked sad but made a point of making a point of smiling, which I found amusing.
