He was a handsome man. He had the look of an ideal father, and he carried himself with dignity. I never saw him angry; his face always wore that sweet smile. I would often see him by the train station with a small bag on his shoulder or a woman in her late thirties carrying two children in a stroller. Sometimes they would have three people, a mother holding a baby, and their daughter walking beside them. This was the most beautiful family I ever witnessed.
That night the driver didn’t make it to the next stop, and my brother got out of the car. We were both upset and started crying
