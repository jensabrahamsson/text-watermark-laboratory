The chair's seat, made of plastic, sagged with time. Its arms were worn away and so were the legs. There was only one thing that could save this chair. It needed its final coat of paint.
My father sat in the middle of the room, looking at me through tired eyes.
"Go for it," he said. "Finish what you started."
I turned the stool around and painted the second leg. My hands moved as if they had a mind of their own; my muscles tensed and relaxed on their own. The chair sagged from where it rested on the floor and cracked at the seams.
