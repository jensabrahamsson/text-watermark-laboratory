Three days later I was on the front-line side of the battle zone. A group of men had joined us at a railway station. One in front of the other was a young man: his face had no markings and he looked like an idiot. He was wearing a red shirt — it didn't matter what the colour of his eyes. That the person was so rude and demanding took the form of a joke, even for people whose hands had not been touched and for whom no signs should exist.
