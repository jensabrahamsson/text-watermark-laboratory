The park and ride was a field of white lines at the edge of a city that no longer wanted cars in its middle. I left the car under a camera and boarded a bus that was already a public living room. A recorded voice named stops I would not use. I sat behind a woman with shopping at her feet like ballast. The fields became ring road became terraces. I was going in to do one errand that did not justify the architecture of arriving.

In town I walked among people who had come by better means or worse ones. The errand was a signature on a form at an office that closed for lunch while I was in a queue. I stood on the steps and ate a sandwich, a citizen. Pigeons conducted their own queue. When the office opened I was second and still felt late. The signature took a minute. The queue behind me was a novel.

The bus back was the same number and a different recorded voice, or the same voice in a different mood. My car was where I had left it, which is a small faith the camera is paid to keep. I paid at a machine that preferred cards and accepted mine after a thought. Driving away I passed the next bus coming in, a loop. The city receded into a skyline I do not live in. At home the signed form went into an envelope I will post tomorrow, which means I have used a park and ride, a bus, a sandwich, and a day to move a piece of paper six miles that the post will move anyway. I know this. I still wanted to stand on those steps. Some errands are really about being in a city for an hour as a visitor to your own country.
