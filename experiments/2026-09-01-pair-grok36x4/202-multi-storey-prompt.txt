The multi-storey car park remembered every tyre that had ever searched for a space. I drove the spiral slower than the painted arrows wanted and found a bay on level four whose lines had been painted twice, neither time matching. The concrete smelled of cold oil. A fluorescent tube flickered in a rhythm I could not ignore once I had noticed it. I sat in the car after switching off because leaving a vehicle in a structure like this always feels slightly like abandoning it.

The lift was out of order on a handwritten sign that had been laminated, which is a kind of permanence. I took the stairs. Each landing had a view of the same brick wall and a different piece of litter. On the street the daylight was a surprise, as if the building had been storing night. I did the errand I had come for, which took twelve minutes and did not deserve the architecture around it. Coming back I miscounted the levels and stood for a while on three, staring at a silver car that was not mine with an intensity that would have worried a passer-by, had there been one.

Level four was still flickering. A woman was loading a pushchair into a boot and swearing at a mechanism designed by someone without children. I found my car by the twice-painted lines. The ticket machine at the exit wanted coins I had spent on the errand. I reversed back up, which the arrows forbade, and borrowed a pound from the woman with the pushchair, who had the face of someone who had borrowed many pounds in car parks. I posted it through and the barrier rose as if it had been in on the joke. Driving out into the rain I felt the spiral still in my wrists.
