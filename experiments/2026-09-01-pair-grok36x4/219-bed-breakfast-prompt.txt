The bed and breakfast had a name that mentioned a view the weather was currently declining to provide. I had booked it because the last train had gone and the alternative was a chair in a station. The landlady showed me a room at the top of stairs that changed their mind halfway. There was a kettle, two biscuits, and a picture of a boat. The mattress remembered other guests. I sat on it and heard the pipes discussing the evening.

Breakfast was in a room of single tables and a radio on low. I was offered a full plate I could not finish and a conversation about the bypass I did not have an opinion on, which did not slow her down. A commercial traveller at the next table buttered toast with the focus of a man who had done this in many towns. Through the window a delivery van blocked the view that the name had promised. I drank tea until it was polite to stop.

I paid at a desk that was also a hall table. She asked if I had slept and I said yes, which was mostly true. The key went into a drawer of other keys. Outside the town was smaller than it had been at night. I walked to the station with my bag and the remaining biscuit in my pocket like a plan. The train came. I found a seat facing backwards and watched the bed and breakfast become a roof among roofs. I have stayed in better rooms and worse ones. This one had a boat on the wall and a woman who cared about breakfast, which is a kind of hospitality that does not need a view. I ate the biscuit in the next county.
