The youth club occupied a hall behind a church that no longer held the numbers it was built for. I was there to help with table tennis because the regular volunteer had a hospital appointment and I can hold a bat. The kitchen hatch sold squash in plastic cups. A stereo played something current I could not name. Teenagers arrived in groups of already-knowing. I was introduced as a helper, which put me in a category with the squash.

I lost several games on purpose and then one by accident, which reset the room. A boy who had not spoken asked if I knew a rule I did, and we stood in the serious weather of explanation. The vicar came through with a box of biscuits and a joke that was older than the stereo. In the corner a girl was painting a banner for a thing I did not catch. Paint on the floor was not a crisis. I liked that. The table tennis ball went under a radiator and three of us lay on the lino to retrieve it, a small expedition.

Closing was a stacking of chairs that felt like the end of a better century. I washed cups. The regular volunteer texted that she was fine. I locked up with a key I had been trusted with for three hours. Walking home I passed the church, dark, and the hall, darker, and felt the particular usefulness of having been in the way in the right way. At the flat I found paint on my cuff. I did not scrub it immediately. Tomorrow I will not be asked again unless I offer, and I might offer, because the ball under the radiator is still, in my mind, a problem worth lying down for.
