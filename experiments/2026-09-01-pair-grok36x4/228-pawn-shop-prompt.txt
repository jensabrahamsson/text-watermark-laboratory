The pawn shop had a window of instruments, cameras, and jewellery that had all been loved at different temperatures. I went in with a watch that had been my father's and was now a weight I did not wear. I told myself I was only asking. The man at the counter had a loupe and a manner that was not unkind. He looked at the watch as an object, which is what I had asked him to do and still found startling. He named a figure. The shop was quiet except for a radio somewhere in the back playing a match.

I did not pawn it. I asked to see a camera I would not buy, to make the visit a browse. He showed me. We talked about film as if either of us still used it. A woman came in with a bag of tools and conducted a different negotiation in a low voice. I looked at the instruments hanging like a paused band. The watch was back on my wrist, heavier for having been priced.

On the street I felt both relieved and as if I had wasted his time. I stood at the window and saw the watch from the outside, on me, among the things that had stayed. A bus threw our reflections across the glass. I went for a walk that had no errand. The figure he had named stayed in my head like a phone number. At home I put the watch in a drawer instead of on the dresser, a compromise with myself. I will not go back next week. I might go back in a year, when the weight has a different name. For now it is a watch that was asked a question and given an answer I did not take.
