The bridge's name "Killer Stoneman" had long been a symbol of destruction in the history of the state's roads because of its location, proximity, and other characteristics. In 1925, the bridge was renamed the "North Fork" and had become the new home of the railroad to the North Fork. At the time, there were at least twenty bridges for the railroad on North Fork in the Union. As a result of the state's decision in 1926 to rebrand the bridges, the North Fork was renamed the East Fork and the East Fork the West Fork.
