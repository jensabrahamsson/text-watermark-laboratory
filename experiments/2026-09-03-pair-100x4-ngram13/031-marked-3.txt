The paper said: "Our findings have implications for the research of whether people are more sensitive to sensory inputs when they are being influenced by food or light cues. However, those who were exposed to increased stress may not have the same sensitivity to food and light as their peers who are not sensitive."

The researchers said it "strongly suggests" that people who are stressed by stimuli might display reduced sensitivity to sensory cues.

The new study also found that people who were more sensitive to food experienced greater levels of fear, aggression, stress and depression when subjected to stress or during periods of deprivation.
