The singers would wear robes and the organist would play a harp. But this time, they sang from an old book and used paper to write down words as they went along.
It was all part of a performance at the Church of St Peter ad Vincula, in the heart of the city of Oxford.
This time the church had been turned into a venue for the Oxford Early Music Festival.
In its programme, the festival aimed to bring back the “authentic sounds” of 16th century music and put the performers in context with the actual instruments used by composers like Thomas Tallis or William Byrd.
