The watch wound by the church. The windings of this watch were regulated by the action of the church clock, which moved at such a rate that it had to be "wound" in order for it to keep time properly with its own movement. (Winds, 30) Here, "windings" refers to how one sets or adjusts something so as to fit within certain limits. In this case, the church's movement was adjusted to move at the correct rate so that it could match the speed of the watch when they met at the same point.
