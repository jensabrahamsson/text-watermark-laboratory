When the ship’s captain arrived for work, he noticed that his clock was running very slow. He called the harbour officer to have it fixed immediately because this would result in him arriving at work late by half an hour! However, when the harbour officer arrived, he told him that he had already adjusted the clock to correct its malfunction; and it was now accurate.
How could the clock be correct and still six minutes fast?
Why did nobody else notice?

This is a classic paradox from logic: the clock being fast means it tells time too early, but when it is corrected it tells time correctly - which is impossible.
