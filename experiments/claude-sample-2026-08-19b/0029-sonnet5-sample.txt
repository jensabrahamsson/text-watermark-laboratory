The corridor smelled of wet wool and coffee from the machine by the stairwell. Students moved in both directions at once, bags catching on shoulders, someone's umbrella dripping a trail across the linoleum. Mikael stood by the noticeboard pretending to read a flyer for a thesis defense he had no intention of attending.

"You're not actually reading that," said Alva, stopping beside him. She had a folder pressed to her chest, the cardboard corners soft from being carried too long.

"I'm considering it."

"You've been considering it for four minutes. I timed you from the water fountain."

He turned. She looked tired in the particular way that came from the library's fluorescent lights, a tiredness that sat under the eyes rather than in them. "How'd the seminar go."

"Bergström asked me to defend a position I don't hold, in front of people I'll have to sit exams with in June." She shifted the folder to her other arm. "So. Normal."

A group pushed past them, laughing about something in a language he didn't recognize, and for a moment the corridor narrowed to just the two of them and the flyer with its curling tape.

"I have twenty minutes before stats," he said. "You want to sit somewhere that isn't this hallway."

"I have the same twenty minutes and a paper due I haven't started."

"That wasn't a no."

She almost smiled, the kind of almost that he'd learned meant yes eventually, not yes now. A radiator somewhere down the hall ticked as it cooled. Outside the tall windows the rain had turned to that fine mist that didn't seem to fall so much as hang there, greying out the parking lot and the row of bicycles chained along the fence.

"Did you finish the reading," she asked, "for tomorrow."

"Half of it. The half that made sense."

"Which half was that."

"The introduction and the conclusion. I'm strategic."

"You're lazy with extra steps."

"That's what strategic means."

A professor came through with a rolling bag, nodding at nobody, and the crowd parted for him the way water parts for a boat, closing again just as fast behind. Alva checked the clock above the stairwell — the kind with a red second hand that jumped rather than swept, always a few seconds behind whatever time actually was.

"I have to go," she said. "Print the paper before Fridriksson locks the office."

"You said you hadn't started it."

"I haven't. But I'll have started it by the time I get there. That's how it works."

She was already moving, folder shifting again, disappearing into the current of people heading for the east stairs. Mikael stayed by the noticeboard a moment longer, long enough to actually read the flyer this time — a defense on morphosyntactic variation in three dialects, two weeks from now, room booked at the far end of a building he'd never once had a class in — before he pulled his own bag higher on his shoulder and went the other way, toward stats, toward the room that always smelled faintly of dry-erase marker no matter the season.
