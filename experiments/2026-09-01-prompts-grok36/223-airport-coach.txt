The airport coach left from a bay behind the station that smelled of other people's early starts. I was not flying. I was meeting a friend who was, a duty of timing I always get slightly wrong. The coach was already a climate of suitcases and quiet. I sat over the wheel because the other seats looked claimed. A recorded voice told us about belts. We pulled out into a dark that was not yet morning and the city became a dual carriageway with its lights still on out of habit.

I dozed and woke at a service station that was not ours. The driver smoked beside the coach with the patience of a timetable. Inside, people bought coffee as if it were a visa. I stayed in my seat and watched a lorry park with the slow dignity of something larger than errands. When we moved again the sky had ideas about blue. Fields appeared, then the long fence of the airport, then the terminals like a city that only believes in departing.

I waited at arrivals holding a sign I was slightly ashamed of, my friend's name in marker as if she were a diplomat. She came through looking like herself plus a different timezone. We hugged among other people's reunions. The coach back was the same vehicle and a different weather. She slept on my shoulder without asking, which is a friendship. I watched the dual carriageway reverse itself. At the station we were ordinary again. I have never liked airports and I like this coach, which is a building that moves and does not ask you to take off your shoes. We ate breakfast in a cafe that had just unlocked. The eggs were ordinary. That was the point.
