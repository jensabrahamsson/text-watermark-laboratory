The night chemist was a hatch in a wall that had been a daytime shop and had drawn itself in after hours. I queued on the pavement with two other people and a moth at the light. When it was my turn I spoke through the glass to a pharmacist who looked as if this hour were her whole profession. I named a prescription that should have been ordinary at four in the afternoon. She disappeared into the bright interior and I watched her move among shelves like a person in an aquarium.

She returned with a bag and questions I answered with the honesty of someone too tired to perform. The card machine worked on the second try. A man behind me coughed in a way that was information. I stepped aside and waited while she served him because leaving immediately felt like abandoning a small night community. The moth continued. A taxi idled as if it knew us.

I walked home along streets that had given up. The bag was a rustle in the quiet. At the flat I read the leaflet as instructed and then not as instructed. The tablets were small and official. I put them by the sink, a lighthouse for the morning. Through the window the chemist hatch was still a square of light on the corner, serving someone I would never see. I am glad it exists. I hope not to know it well. Tonight I did, briefly, and the moth and the taxi and the cough are now part of the medicine, which is not how the leaflet puts it, and is still true.
