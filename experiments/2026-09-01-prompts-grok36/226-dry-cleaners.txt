The dry cleaners had a bell that announced you as a problem of fabric. I brought a coat I had worn to the funeral and then to too many other days. A man behind the counter wrote on a ticket in a script that was almost a drawing. He asked about stains and I pointed to a map of my year. He nodded as if stains were a language he preferred to English. The shop smelled of heat and chemicals and other people's Fridays.

I was given a yellow ticket and a date. Behind him garments rotated slowly in their plastic, a private fashion show. A woman came in with a suit and a story about a wedding. I stood aside and looked at the price list, which was a philosophy of care. When it was my turn again he had already finished writing. I paid in cash because the card machine was a guest that had not arrived. The ticket went into my wallet beside the cemetery form, an accidental archive.

On the collection day the coat came back looking like a relative who had slept. I put it on in the shop to check the sleeves, a small ritual. It smelled of the shop, not of the funeral, which was the point and also a kind of forgetting I had paid for. Walking home I felt overdressed for the pavement. At the flat I hung it up and the plastic stayed on for a day because taking it off felt like unwrapping a decision. I took it off. The coat is just a coat again. The yellow ticket I threw away, then retrieved, then threw away properly. Some receipts are allowed to end.
