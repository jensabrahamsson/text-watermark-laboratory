The squash courts were behind the old baths, a corridor of doors with windows set high so you could see only the top of a game. I had been booked in by a friend who needed a body to hit the ball back. The hire shoes were a size that was nearly right. We warmed up by missing. The court was a white box that made every sound into a report. I sweated immediately, a talent.

He was better. I ran anyway, which is my contribution to most sports. A let was called that I did not understand and then did. We played to a number I lost track of. Between games we sat against the wall and drank from a bottle we shared without hygiene, an old friendship. Someone in the next court was shouting at himself in a useful way. I thought of the leisure centre gym and preferred this box, which at least admitted it was a fight.

Afterwards the shower was the same two temperatures as every municipal shower. I dressed with arms that had opinions. He asked if I wanted a regular slot. I said I would look at the diary, which is a drawer where nos live. We walked out past the baths, closed, a building remembering water. I was hungry in a simple way. At the flat I ate standing up and felt the day's geometry in my knees. I will play again if he asks twice. Once is an invitation; twice is a court. I put the hire-shoe blister plaster in the bin and kept the feeling of the white box, a room that told the truth about effort.
