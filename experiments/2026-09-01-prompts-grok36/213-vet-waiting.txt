The veterinary waiting room had a tank of fish that had survived many other people's afternoons. I sat with a cat carrier on my knees. The cat had stopped complaining, which I took as a bad sign and then as a good one and then as nothing I could use. A dog the size of a coat was being told it was brave. Magazines on the table were about gardens and famous people, neither of which applied. The receptionist called names in a voice trained not to alarm animals.

When it was our turn the cat became a different animal, all muscle and opinion. I apologised to the nurse as if I had invented cats. The room was a steel table and a poster about teeth. The vet was younger than I expected and more tired than the poster. She felt along the cat's side and named a possibility I had already googled at two in the morning. We agreed on blood and a wait. I held the scruff as instructed and felt like a traitor and a nurse at once.

Back in the waiting room the fish continued their work. I bought a pouch of food I did not need because the shelf was there and I was a person who needed to buy something. A child asked if my cat was dying and her mother said shush and I said no, which was a hope dressed as a fact. We were called for the results on a piece of paper that used words I would later look up again. The cat slept in the carrier on the bus home, a small heat against my leg. I sat with the paper folded so other passengers would not become involved. At the flat I opened the carrier and the cat walked out as if the day had been my idea.
