"Third one this month," Dev said, coiling the hose back onto its rack. "Mrs. Halloran's toaster again?"

"Her smoke detector's just tired." Marta wiped down the dash where someone's coffee had sloshed during the run. "Like the rest of us."

Dev laughed, low and rough from adrenaline still leaving his blood. He was nineteen, still new enough that his hands shook a little after every call, false alarm or not. Marta had stopped noticing the shake in her own hands years ago — not because it went away, but because she'd made peace with it, the way you make peace with weather.

They worked in the comfortable silence of people who'd done this a hundred times together. Marta checked the oxygen tanks, tapping each gauge like a doctor checking a pulse. Dev swept the floor where boot prints had tracked in from the wet grass, the broom scraping in long, unhurried strokes.

"You ever think about how many of these are real?" Dev asked. "Like, actually real. Fire real."

"Enough to keep doing this." Marta closed the tank cabinet, the latch clicking home. "Not enough that you should be disappointed when they're not."

"I'm not disappointed." He paused, broom resting against his palms. "Okay, maybe a little."

"When I started," Marta said, "I wanted the big one too. The one you tell stories about at retirement dinners. Took me a few years to understand that a quiet station is the whole point. We're not here to be heroes. We're here so nobody needs one."

Dev didn't say anything, but he nodded like it landed somewhere.

They finished in the particular rhythm of a small station — checking equipment, logging the call, refilling the water in the break room carafe for whoever came in tomorrow morning. Marta noted the time in the logbook: 2:47 AM, false alarm, smoke detector malfunction, no injuries. Her handwriting was neat, unhurried, the same for a kitchen fire as for nothing at all.

Outside, the town was dark except for the gas station on the corner and a porch light two houses down that had been left on by accident or by someone waiting for a call that wasn't coming.

Dev killed the interior lights one bank at a time, the building dimming in sections like something falling asleep. Marta checked the side door, the back door, the big bay doors last, watching them shudder down with a metal groan that always sounded, to her, like the building sighing.

"Same time next week?" Dev asked, jingling his keys.

"Same time whenever the phone rings," Marta said, locking the front. "That's the job."

They stood a moment in the parking lot, the way people do when a night has asked something of them and they're not quite ready to separate. Then Dev got in his car, and Marta got in hers, and the station sat behind them, dark and ready, waiting for the next thing that probably wouldn't be anything at all.
