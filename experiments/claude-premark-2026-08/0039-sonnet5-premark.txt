The corridor smelled of instant coffee and wet umbrellas. Fluorescent light buzzed over rows of lockers, and the tide of students between lectures pushed past in two directions, backpacks knocking against elbows.

Elin found a gap by the noticeboard and leaned there, scrolling through nothing in particular, when Aron appeared out of the crowd with his usual half-run, half-walk.

"You didn't answer my message," he said, slightly out of breath.

"I did. I said I didn't know."

"You said 'maybe,' which is not the same as 'I don't know.' Maybe means you're leaning yes."

"Maybe means maybe." She closed her phone and looked at him properly. "What's this actually about? The study group or something else?"

He shifted his bag to the other shoulder, buying himself half a second. "The study group. Obviously."

"Obviously," she repeated, unconvinced, but let it go.

A group of first-years surged past them, loud about something from the lecture — a professor's comment about determinism that had apparently offended someone's entire worldview. One of them was gesturing wildly, nearly hitting a passing TA with a rolled-up handout.

"I actually liked that lecture," Elin said, watching them go. "The bit about causal chains. Made me think about my thesis differently."

"You always say that. Every lecture 'makes you think about your thesis differently.' At some point you have to just write the thing."

"I am writing the thing."

"You're writing around the thing. There's a difference." He said it lightly, but there was an edge under it, the kind that comes from someone who has read too many of your half-finished drafts.

She didn't answer right away. Down the corridor, a door opened and a wave of new noise spilled out — chairs scraping, someone's laugh too loud for the hour, the smell of chalk dust and old radiators.

"Fine," she said finally. "I'll come to the study group. But I'm not doing the presentation part again. Last time you both just sat there watching me improvise for twenty minutes."

"That's because you're good at it."

"That's not a compliment, it's exploitation."

He grinned, and for a second the earlier edge disappeared entirely. "Seven, then? Library, the room with the broken blinds."

"The one that's always too hot?"

"That's the one."

A bell — or whatever passed for a bell in this building, a dull electronic tone — sounded somewhere above them, and the corridor's rhythm changed, everyone suddenly remembering they had somewhere to be. Aron was already backing away, walking backwards for a few steps the way he always did, like he couldn't fully commit to leaving a conversation.

"Seven," he said again, as if repeating it would make it true.

"Seven," Elin agreed, and watched him turn and vanish into the current of bodies heading toward the stairwell, before she pushed off the wall and went the other way, back into the noise.
