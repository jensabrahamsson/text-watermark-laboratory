3. Plan to live within your means.
Heading into work late on Thursday, an unforeseen situation might come up. A hotel room is available. Lunch and dinner are included. Payment is expected from you. Without funds, you're free to handle it however you see fit. You'll be welcomed at the hotel and can switch rooms whenever something feels inconvenient or uncomfortable.

4. If you're unsure, reach out to your manager.
Simply ask what he'll tell you that evening. If he doesn't respond, request a callback. He's typically the one trying to walk you through issues you don't fully grasp, or he might be the one at fault for not securing you the ideal position — but there's still a chance things work out and you return to your job. Sometimes matters weighing on you are better discussed outside the hotel setting, something he might not be comfortable authorizing. It may be wise not to mention that he's allowed to phone you back.

5. Hold off on settling what you owe.
There are times when clearing your debts makes sense — typically covering your credit card balance and a mortgage payment or two — especially with someone you trust to help recover your funds so you can get back to your job. If you're uncertain about money you owe, ask your supervisor to return the call, or check whether reimbursement is possible.

6. Keep up with your payments.
Should your boss note that you're running late for work, then you are indeed late. He might mention that he himself is frequently late, which is apparently acceptable for him, and he'll continue asking how long you've been waiting while reminding you that you'd like him to arrange coverage so you can return another day or the following one. If guilt over an unpaid bill sets in, he could ask whether anyone who notices your tardiness gets to address it — and that's something you might come to regret.
