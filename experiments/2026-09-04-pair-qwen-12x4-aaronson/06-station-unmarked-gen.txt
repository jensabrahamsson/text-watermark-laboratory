The two boys in front of me were both in high school, but their parents couldn’t agree on what college they should attend. One boy said he wanted to go to Harvard or Yale. The other wanted MIT or Stanford. But their parents wouldn’t let them pick any specific school; instead, they had agreed that either would do. They stood there at the ticket window while the woman behind me pressed her hands against her face and sighed loudly. In this city, people are all over thirty-five now. They’re no longer teenagers. Yet they still haven’t reached full adulthood. They’ve only just started getting jobs.
