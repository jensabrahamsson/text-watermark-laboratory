And so the children of my village passed through that building. Each of us looked at each other and had a vague feeling of disappointment.

There, in the street, the people in their twenties, their faces covered with wrinkles from the years, looked back and forth on a wide street that became my village: the quiet and quiet-ish village that had once been the home of my two daughters and, now, that of my mother and of my father and his children.
