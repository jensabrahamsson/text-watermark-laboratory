"Oh, my God," he said, and looked around.

I turned my head toward an alley behind me, and saw another baker sitting at a crosswalk, his head bowed from his gaze.

"I was right at your bakery," he said.

The door swung open and I stood next to the doorkeeper.

She shrugged, and said, "It's fine," and followed behind to the back.

I looked down at the loaf. It was not a loaf of butter; this was a loaf of bread. It was butter.

I opened it, and found it was made by a Polish baker, named Duska.

"And what's it made?" a voice asked from behind Duska, a tall, round man dressed in a yellow-draped shirt and hat.

"You're the only baker in my bakery," he said. "I just bought you from a friend in the city. Can I ask you a favor?"

Duska turned back to me just in time to see me, still smiling, and gave me the first good look at what was taking place in the corner next to, and beyond, the bakery.
