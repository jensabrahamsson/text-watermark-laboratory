"This is the scene we had all been waiting for," said Jim, 35. "It was very surreal. But we all stayed there for a little bit at least."

At about 2 p.m., as hundreds of hikers and photographers swarmed the park, a small stream ran down the right side of the road, blocking the road to the shore.

Traffic became so heavy that they could hardly be heard by some of the park's hundreds of volunteers.

Jim, who's lived at the park for 19 years, had stopped at the lake to take a walk, looking south along the edge of the stream.

"I was pretty afraid. And I was very brave," he said.

The park, built in 1912, is the first public recreation project in the San Francisco state, and has attracted millions of visitors for more than a month.

It began in 1906 as a recreational and environmental program for the Golden Gate National Forest, which would run through the summer and fall, and continued for nearly 30 years. Over the last two decades, the park has attracted a variety of local, national and international interests for the past decades.
