"Whoop!" the third guy yelled out loud, jumping the window and landing on the train. There was no car there. The train continued on its next trip. But even with the help of a couple of people, I couldn't say a word about the car.

I turned my head toward an alley behind me, and saw another old man leaning on the curb that had been on the same journey that took the bus. I took his name as he told me to take the money back to a few days in advance and get the money I needed.

I picked up the money and ran to my hotel.

"Come in the bus," said another older boy, taking my purse out of my hand. "Do it." He looked at me suspiciously.

"You're the only one doing it," I repeated in a voice that seemed to be a combination of irritation and anxiety.

His eyes widened like he'd been attacked by something strong. "Don't get me wrong, you're not alone here, especially given all the people and agencies that work these jobs. But I had good luck in your city. I was in Brooklyn, I came to America and got to work with a nice old woman named Elizabeth.
