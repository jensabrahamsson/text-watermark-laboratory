The boxing gym was a hall with a clock that nobody trusted and a ring that took up more of the floor than seemed fair. Bags hung in a line like coats that had decided to stay. I had been told to come at ten because the serious people trained earlier. The door stuck. Inside, a radio talked about football and a man skipped rope as if the rope were a sentence he had memorised. I signed a form that asked for an emergency name and wrote my own, which the woman at the desk allowed with a look that said she had seen worse planning.

The warm-up was shamefully ordinary: jogging the edge of the hall, touching a line, turning. Someone lent me wraps and talked me through the thumb. My hands became parcels. On the bag I landed nothing that would have mattered in a story and a few things that made a decent sound. The coach said less and that was useful. Sweat went into my eyes. I learned the distance at which my own arms surprised me.

Afterwards I sat on a stacked mat and drank water from a tap that tasted of pipes. The serious people arrived as I was leaving, which felt correct. The door stuck again on the way out. On the street the air was colder than the hall by a season. I unwrapped my hands on the wall outside and the cloth came off in a spiral, slightly damp, smelling of soap that was not mine. I put the wraps in a plastic bag the desk had given me unasked. Walking home I kept making a fist and releasing it, as if the bag and the clock and the rope sentence were still deciding whether I would come back at ten, or earlier, or not at all.
