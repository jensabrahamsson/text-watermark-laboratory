After a long pause, I am back in my apartment. I don't want to do it again.

This is the first time I went down the main pathway, over a creek. The river ran over the building before I had the chance, and I had to get up from the front window, into the river. Even now, I can look at the brick pavement while it passes over my roof. I was walking by the car before. I have lost touch of the old canal that I walked through in my twenties. The wind was heavy now, the air cold now, the river rippling over the trees. I think it is all because water had evaporated.

I cannot talk of another way it came down. I have never had rain like this on my street or on my apartment wall, but I have seen it rain. I have seen the car and the people come on the sidewalk and walk about. I have seen all the rivers passing by. I have watched them go by. I have seen how the road goes all back up and into the river. The one day you don't even bother to talk about it, there was nothing the car could do anymore. You just sit there waiting, and never think about it. That is my world and you try to explain it. Then the road comes back. The thing makes you want to keep going. It's a miracle everything works smoothly. It's a miracle.

Even in New York my family doesn't have an apartment. Their kids didn't have one. The old man's brother was a teacher for eight years; everybody was from school. I couldn't afford much for my family, but even so, the things were worth it. He had a wife, three kids and two grand. He could have bought a house but had bought it with a lot more money. He had a friend, who was older and more educated, who was older. He got his money without paying rent. He did as much as his parents could with it. But he lost a couple kids. I've seen and listened to all the people on his side for twenty-five years. He went all over them. I just didn't see what they were doing. He was not at the center of his family. He was only one of twenty people but he didn't have much to say about it. Nobody knew he was really rich. He just didn't know what the world was really like. Everyone in the city was just poor.

A few months before I was born I read that I could buy four cars. One was good, I thought, but my brother didn't say anything. But the next day, when I heard it was not that good, and that they would not give me a car, I realized it was the same. As I drove over the old river, in the middle of the night, I never said my whole name or said anything I couldn't remember. I was only trying to understand what my life was like, like being on my own. What would go on there? What would I want to call it? A world gone by, for no one, I told myself. But I kept coming back for more and more of the people in the town. I didn't do anything for them.
