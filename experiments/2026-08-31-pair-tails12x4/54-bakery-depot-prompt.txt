At three in the morning the bakery on Cross Street is only ovens and a radio. The first tray of rolls comes out before the streetlights dim. Flour sits in the grooves of the wooden bench like a map of last night. At the depot loop the driver opens both doors and eats something from a foil tray. Two minutes. Then we go back the other way with fewer people. I always think I will walk the last stretch and I never do. The stop nearest my street has no shelter. You stand under the tree anyway, because that is what the others do, and the bus appears exactly when you have decided it will not.
