The corridor smelled of wet wool and coffee from the machine by the stairs. Fluorescent tubes buzzed overhead, one of them flickering at the far end where a maintenance sticker had peeled halfway off. Students moved in both directions at once, bags scraping against the radiators that lined the wall, and somewhere a door slammed hard enough that a noticeboard shuddered and dropped one of its thumbtacked flyers to the floor.

Elin stood by the vending machine, feeding coins into the slot for the second time because the first attempt had eaten her money without dispensing anything.

"It does that," said a voice behind her. A guy with a canvas bag over one shoulder, sleeves pushed up past his elbows, ink stain on his wrist. "You have to hit it. Not hard. Just — here."

He rapped his knuckle twice against the plastic housing, low, near the coin return. The machine clicked and a bottle of water dropped.

"That's not mine," she said.

"No, but you can have it. I don't even want water." He handed it over anyway, and she took it because refusing seemed like more effort than it was worth.

"Kristoffer," he said. "Second year. You're in the Wednesday seminar, the one with Lindqvist."

"Elin. And yes. You sit in the back."

"I sit in the back because Lindqvist calls on people in the front and I haven't done the reading in three weeks."

She laughed, short, more air than sound, and looked past him at the clock above the lecture hall doors. Eleven minutes until the next one started.

"You should do the reading," she said. "It's not long. Forty pages."

"Forty pages of what, though. He keeps assigning things and none of them ever come up in the exam questions. I checked last year's exam. Compared it to the syllabus. There's maybe thirty percent overlap."

"You did that instead of the reading."

"I did that instead of the reading."

The corridor thinned as people filtered into rooms, doors closing one after another down the row, the noise dropping in stages until it was mostly just the vending machine's compressor and the flickering tube. A girl ran past them with a laptop clutched to her chest like it might otherwise fall and shatter, calling an apology over her shoulder to no one in particular.

"Aren't you going in," Kristoffer said, nodding toward the door behind her.

"In a minute."

"Lindqvist locks the door at five past."

"I know. He's done it to me before."

He shifted the bag on his shoulder, seemed like he might say something else, didn't. The tube at the end of the corridor gave up flickering and simply stayed dark, and neither of them looked at it. She unscrewed the cap on the water bottle, drank some, screwed it back on.

"Well," she said.

"Well," he said, and went the other way, toward the stairwell, without saying where.

She stood another moment, then pushed through the door with four minutes left on the clock.
