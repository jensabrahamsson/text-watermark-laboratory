The library on a wet Tuesday is quieter than people who do not use libraries expect. Not silent. Chairs scrape. A printer in the back room complains on every third page. Someone in local history whispers into a phone about a missing baptism record from 1912. The staff move like people who have already had the argument about food at the desks and have decided not to have it again today.

I came for a map of the old canal and stayed because the radiator under the window works. The map is a photocopy in a folder with a pencil note: do not fold. The canal is a car park now. You can still see the curve in the street if you already know it is there. A boy at the next table copies dates into a school book and then draws a small dog in the margin, very carefully, as if the dog were also a date.

Closing time is announced twice. The second time people actually stand. I put the folder back in the wrong place and then in the right one. Outside the rain has thinned. The stone steps are dark and the buses throw yellow light across them. I do not have the canal. I have an hour I did not plan to spend, which is close enough.
