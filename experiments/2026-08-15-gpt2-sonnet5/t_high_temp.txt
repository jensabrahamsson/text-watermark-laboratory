3. Be ready to spend less than you've earned.


As you head to work late Thursday, you may be faced with an unexpected task. A hotel is open. You have lunch and dinner. You are expected to pay. If you have no money, you can do whatever you want. You're welcome into your hotel and are entitled to change hotel rooms every time you do something inconvenient or awkward.


4. When in doubt, call your manager.


Just ask what he is going to say to you at night. If he doesn't, ask him to call back. He's the one who is always trying to explain you problems you don't understand or that he is the one responsible for failing to get you the best job possible and you might be able to make it and get back to work. Sometimes, things that are on your mind are better off outside of the hotel, which he or she may not give you permission to tell him about. Maybe don't tell him he can call you back.


5. Don't pay what you owe.


Sometimes, it's a good idea to pay what you owe -- usually the amount you owe from your credit card and a couple of mortgage payments from you -- to someone you are sure will help you get your money back so you can return to work. If you owe money without knowing, ask your boss to call him back or ask if you can get your payment back.


6. Pay your bills.


If your boss says you are getting late to work, you are getting late. He or she could tell you that your boss is always there late, which is fine for him though, and he or she will keep asking you how long you waited and keep reminding you that you want him to find a replacement for you so you can come back to work on another day or the next day. If you're feeling bad about not having paid your bill, he may ask if everyone who sees you late gets to pick it up, and you may regret it later.
