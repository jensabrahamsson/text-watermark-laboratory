Thought for 1s

Dear Marit,

I am writing this from a plastic seat in Hallsberg station, which is not where I meant to be. The train left Stockholm at 23:40 and I fell asleep somewhere after Katrineholm, and when I woke the woman across from me was gone and the light outside had changed from black to the flat grey that comes before real morning. I checked my phone. We had passed Falköping forty minutes earlier.

I want to tell you it was dramatic, waking like that, but it wasn't. It was just wrong in a quiet way, the way a word sounds wrong when you say it too many times. I sat for a while with my bag on my knees before I did anything. There was a conductor's voice on the intercom announcing something about a delay further down the line, unrelated to me, and I remember feeling almost grateful for it, like the train had its own problems and mine could wait.

Hallsberg at five in the morning is mostly closed. There is a kiosk that opens at six. I stood outside it and watched a man in an orange vest walk the length of the platform checking something on each car, tapping the metal with a small hammer, the sound arriving a half second after I saw his arm move. I don't know why that struck me the way it did. Maybe because it was the only thing happening that had a clear purpose.

I thought about calling you. I had the phone out and your name up and then I put it away again. I'm not sure what I would have said. Not "come get me," it's four hours by car and you have Elsa's recital today. Not "I'm fine," because that would have needed explaining why I was telling you at all. I think I just wanted someone to know where I was for a few minutes, before I fixed it myself, which is not the same as needing help.

The kiosk opened. I bought a coffee that was worse than the coffee on the train, and a paper I didn't read. The board said there was a train back through at 06:52, and another one after that continuing to where I was actually supposed to go, arriving only ninety minutes later than planned. So the whole thing amounts to ninety minutes, in the end. It is strange how a night can feel bent out of shape and the arithmetic afterward is so small.

I keep thinking about the sound of that hammer on the wheels, checking for something that would only show itself as a wrongness in the ring of the metal. I don't have a point to make about it. I just wanted to describe it to someone before the memory smooths over, the way these things do, into "I missed my stop once and had to wait around."

I'll be home by early afternoon. Don't change anything on my account.

J.
