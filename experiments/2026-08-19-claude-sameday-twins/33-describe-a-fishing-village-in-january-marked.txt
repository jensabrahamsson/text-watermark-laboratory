Thought for 2s

January comes down on the harbor like a lid. The water is the color of an old coin, and the boats sit lower than they did in autumn, weighted with rain that's collected under the tarps. Nobody's bailed them out yet. There's no rush.

The fish houses along the quay have their doors shut, but smoke still comes up thin from two or three chimneys, the ones belonging to men who haven't given up mending gear for the season. Inside, if you looked, you'd find nets spread over trestles, needles worked through torn mesh, a radio turned low to a station nobody's really listening to. The smell is diesel and brine and wet wool.

Most of the fleet is tied up. Four boats, five, hulls streaked orange-brown where the rust has bled down from the scuppers. A gull stands on the wheelhouse of the nearest one, not doing anything, just standing, feathers puffed against the cold. The halyards knock against the masts of the two sailboats still in the water, an uneven clacking that stops and starts with the wind.

The main street is short. A shop, closed on weekdays before ten, a church with a squat tower, a war memorial with three names on it going soft at the edges from a hundred winters. Nobody is out. A woman crosses from the shop to her car with a bag in each hand, walking fast, head down against the wind coming off the water. She doesn't look at the harbor. People who live by it stop seeing it after a while, the way you stop hearing a clock.

Down past the last house, where the road turns to gravel, there's a slipway with a winch nobody's used since November. The chain has gone orange. Beyond it, the breakwater, and beyond that, gray going into gray, no clean line where the sea stops and the sky starts. A trawler passes far out, not one of the local boats, too big for this harbor, moving without hurry toward some other port.

The cold isn't dramatic. It's just there, in the wet stone, in the damp that gets into a coat no matter how it's buttoned. By three in the afternoon the light is already going, the streetlamp by the shop flickering once before it holds. Someone's dog barks twice from behind a fence and stops.

At the end of the quay, an old man sits inside a parked car with the engine off, watching nothing in particular. Maybe waiting for someone. Maybe just sitting because the house is smaller than the view. He doesn't roll down the window. After a while he starts the engine, and the car pulls out slowly, and the harbor goes back to being just water and boats and the gull, still standing on the wheelhouse, not moving, as the light drops another degree toward evening.
