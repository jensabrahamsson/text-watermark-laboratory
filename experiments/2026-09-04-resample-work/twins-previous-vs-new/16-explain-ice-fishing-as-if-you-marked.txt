Thought for 2s

Someone's uncle had a truck with a heater that worked in theory. That's how it started. We drove out before it was light, which felt important at the time, like the fish would know if we slept in. The lake had a name I don't remember and a surface that looked solid from the shore and less solid the further out we walked.

The hole itself takes longer to make than you'd think, or it did with the auger we had, which was dull and needed two people to keep straight. My hands stopped being useful somewhere around hole three. Not frostbite, nothing dramatic, just that stiff uselessness where your fingers still bend but don't report back on what they're doing.

Then you sit. That's the part nobody mentions when they talk about ice fishing like it's an event. You drilled a hole, you drop a line in it, and now you sit on an overturned bucket for four hours in a wind that comes off the ice with nothing to slow it down. The uncle had a folding chair with a cushion and didn't offer to trade.

I caught one fish. It was small enough that even the uncle, who wanted us to be having a good time, looked at it and didn't say much. We put it back. I don't think either of us believed it would survive the return trip, but the alternative was cleaning a fish that size in that wind, and nobody wanted that.

What I remember most isn't the fish or the hole or even the cold exactly, but the specific boredom of it — a boredom with nothing to do about it, because you can't walk around, you can't really talk over the wind, you just watch a six-inch circle of black water and wait for something to happen to it. The thermos ran out before noon. Someone had brought crackers that were fine and then, an hour later, were the only thing that mattered.

Around one o'clock a different group further out started packing up, and watching them do it was its own small event — figures in orange moving around, sleds being loaded, an engine turning over that didn't catch the first three times. We left not long after. Nobody suggested staying.

In the truck the heater caught up with itself about ten minutes from home, which felt like a joke at the time, though I don't remember finding it funny then. I remember thinking I'd try it again sometime with better boots, and then not doing that. I still don't know if the appeal is supposed to be the fishing or the sitting or the being-a-person-who-does-this, and I never stayed out there long enough, or often enough, to find out. The lake is probably still there. I assume people are still catching things I didn't.
