After a while, my dad sat down to lunch on the porch swing; he’s always liked to sit there when the weather is good. The sky was still dark but white, as if it were holding back its tears; the stars twinkled and swirled like smoke rings. I felt a cold wind come up the hill. “It’ll be okay,” my mom said. I looked out and saw the river under moonlight, so black it glowed.
I went inside to work on the essay about the woman who lived here before us—her name and her face, her story, and how we’d all known each other
