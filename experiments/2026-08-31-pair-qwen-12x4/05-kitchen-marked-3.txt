After a while, my dad came home from work in a blue shirt. He asked me to read him his mail from his briefcase. There were no letters or postcards that morning, only two bills. “How’s school?” he said. It’s not important anymore, I said, like how you didn’t go to the park after all, because your brother is in jail for killing someone, just like that, in front of the house where we used to live, so nobody has ever seen the place since, but I could still remember where everything was.
