The last stop is always the quiet one. Not silent, exactly—the bus still ticks and hums, the doors still hiss when they open—but quiet in a different way than the morning run, when everyone's half asleep and staring at their phones like the screens might wake them up faster than coffee. By nine at night the bus has emptied out slow, one or two people at a time, until it's just me and maybe a guy in the back seat who rode past his stop because he fell asleep, and I have to say his name over the mic, or just say "hey, buddy, end of the line," and watch him jolt up like he doesn't know where he is for a second.

My feet know this route better than my head does at this point. Nine hours behind the wheel and my hands still move before I think about it—signal, mirror, brake, the little rhythm of it. I've driven the same eleven miles so many times I could probably do it with my eyes closed, which is a scary thing to admit out loud but it's true for anybody who's done this long enough. You stop seeing the street and start feeling it instead. You know which manhole cover makes the bus shudder, which light always runs a beat longer than it should, which corner the wind comes around and pushes at you like it's trying to have a conversation.

By the last loop the city looks different too. Storefronts are dark except for the ones that never close, the laundromat with its flickering sign, the gas station lit up too bright like it's trying to make up for everyone else going dim. People who get on this late are a different crowd than the morning ones. Nurses coming off shift. Kids who probably shouldn't be out this late but are. A woman with grocery bags who thanks me every single time like I did something more than just open a door for her, and honestly, some nights that's the thing that gets me through the last hour.

There's a particular kind of tired that settles in around stop forty, forty-one, somewhere in there. Not sleepy tired. More like your whole body's been holding a shape all day and it wants to remember it can be a different shape. My shoulders ache in that spot they always ache. My eyes feel like they've been open too long even though they have been, technically, the exact amount of time they're supposed to be.
