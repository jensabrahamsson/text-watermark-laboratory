Claude responded: Kära Elin,

Kära Elin,

The storm has been at the glass for six days now. I say "at the glass" because that is how it feels up here—not wind around a building but wind against a face, one continuous face, the lamp room. I have stopped trying to read at night. The pages move even when the door is shut and the window latched. I hold the book down with the coffee tin.

Tuesday the supply boat did not come. I did not expect it. Wednesday I ate the last of the good bread and started on the hard biscuit, which I have never liked, and put butter on it anyway. You would say that is a small complaint to write to a person who has three children in a flat with no working stove, and you would be right, so I will not dwell on the biscuit.

The gulls disappeared on the second day. I noticed because I always notice them, more than I notice most people, and their absence made the rock feel larger, as if something had been subtracted from a drawing and the paper underneath showed through. On the fourth day I found two of them dead against the base of the tower, blown there, wings still spread the way they spread them to turn. I did not bury them. There is nowhere to bury anything here. I put them over the seaward wall.

The light itself has not failed, which is the only thing that matters in the log I keep for the authority, and so the log says: light operational, fuel adequate, no incidents. I do not write in the log that on Thursday night I sat on the floor of the lamp room for an hour because standing had become tiring in a way I could not explain, or that I talked to Father for a while, out loud, which I have not done since before you were married. He did not answer, in case you were wondering. I am not going strange out here, or if I am, I am going strange slowly enough that I would not be the one to notice it first.

Today the wind dropped by half, which the barometer says means nothing yet, but I choose to believe it anyway. I have hung my wet things by the stove and they steam like something alive. I can see, for the first time since Sunday, the shape of the mainland, grey on grey, and a single light that might be Karlsson's house or might be a boat, I cannot tell which and it does not matter which.

Tell Bengt the radio still works, in case he was asking. Tell the children I have not been eaten by anything, though the biscuit came close to eating me. I will send this with whatever boat comes first, and if none comes for another week you will simply have to trust that the light is still turning, because it is, because that is the one thing here that does not stop for weather.

Din bror,
Anders
