Dear M.,

I am writing this from a station whose name I did not know existed until forty minutes ago, on a bench that smells of rain and diesel, waiting for a train that will take me back the way I came. I missed my stop. I want to tell you about it before the ordinariness of the explanation catches up with the feeling of it, because the feeling was, for a while, something close to grace.

I had been sitting by the window since Malmö, watching the fields go from visible to inferred to nothing at all, and somewhere past midnight I stopped fighting the tiredness in my eyes. I don't think I fully slept. It was more like the train borrowed my attention for a while and gave it back changed. When I opened my eyes fully we were slowing into a station with a name I didn't recognize, all sodium light and empty platform, and it took me a full thirty seconds of looking at the sign to understand that this was not almost my stop, this was two stops past it.

What I want to describe is not the inconvenience — though there was inconvenience, and a conductor who was kind about it in the specific weary way of someone who has explained connecting trains four hundred times. What I want to describe is the ten minutes before that, still asleep or nearly so, when the train was going somewhere and I did not know where and did not, for those minutes, especially care. There is a particular peace in being carried past your intention. All day I move through lists — yours, mine, the ones neither of us wrote but obey anyway — and then the train simply declined to stop where the list said it should, and some old part of me that has been waiting a long time for permission sat up and said: good.

I keep thinking about how much of my life is a series of stops I did not miss. I got off where I was supposed to. I have been, by most measures, an excellent passenger. And yet here I am, unreasonably moved by forty extra minutes of unscheduled dark, by the shape of a country I wasn't looking for, by a station attendant's flashlight swinging on the wet platform like something out of a film I'd have called overwrought if I'd seen it rather than lived it.

I'll be home later than planned. You'll have gone to bed, probably, and I'll let myself in quietly and you won't wake, or you will and you'll mumble something about the time and I'll say the train was late, which is true and also not the truth I want to give you, which is this letter, which is: I missed my stop and for a little while I was nowhere in particular, going somewhere unnamed, and it was the most rested I've felt in months.

I'm on the platform now. The next train is in six minutes. I'll be home by morning.

With love, and a little dirt from an unfamiliar station on my shoes,

J.
