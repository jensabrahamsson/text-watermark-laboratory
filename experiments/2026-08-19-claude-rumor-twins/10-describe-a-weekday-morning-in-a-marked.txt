The shutter comes up at ten past seven, later than it should, because the key sticks in the cold and Torbjörn has to jiggle it with his knee against the frame. Inside it smells like machine oil and cardboard and the particular mustiness of a building that's been heated and cooled too many times. He doesn't turn on all the lights at once. Just the front, then the counter, then he walks back through the aisles flipping switches as he goes, and the fluorescent tubes buzz and stutter before they catch.

The delivery from the wholesaler is already there, dropped sometime before six, boxes stacked against the door so he has to shoulder it open. Screws, mostly. A crate of paint thinner that's leaking slightly at one corner, just enough to smell it before he sees it, and he swears under his breath and goes looking for a rag. The invoice doesn't match the boxes — never does — three items short and one he didn't order, a case of caulking guns nobody asked for. He leaves it by the register to deal with later, which means it'll sit there until Thursday.

The coffee maker in the back room takes forever, and while it's going he counts the till, twice, because the first count comes out four kronor off and he can't be bothered to figure out where. He writes it down anyway. A notebook, not a spreadsheet, spiral-bound and swollen with damp, sitting under the counter next to a jar of loose washers nobody's sure the purpose of anymore.

First customer is Ingvar, same as every weekday, in for nothing in particular — a packet of sandpaper, maybe, or just to stand by the paint counter and talk about his gutters. He's got a theory about the gutters he's been developing for three weeks. Torbjörn listens with about sixty percent of his attention while restocking wood screws into the wrong bins because the right ones are still in a box somewhere.

A woman comes in around eight-thirty needing a specific kind of hinge for a cabinet door, holds up her phone with a blurry photo, and they go back and forth for a while before landing on something that's probably close enough. She buys two, in case.

The floor by the key-cutting machine is gritty with brass shavings that never quite get swept up, just pushed around. A radio on the shelf behind the counter plays something local, low, mostly ignored except when the weather comes on. Outside the window a delivery truck idles too long, exhaust fogging up against the glass, and somebody's dog is tied to the bike rack, patient, watching the door.

By nine the leaking thinner has soaked through the rag and started on the cardboard underneath, and Torbjörn finally deals with it properly, hauling the crate out back where it sits, still leaking a little, next to the caulking guns nobody ordered and a stack of flattened boxes waiting for a recycling pickup that keeps getting pushed to next week.
