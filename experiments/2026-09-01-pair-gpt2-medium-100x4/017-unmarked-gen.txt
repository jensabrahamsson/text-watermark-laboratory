On Wednesday morning, Mr. Shulman and his assistant began looking into why his colleague's name appeared twice on the ledger.

By midday Thursday, Mr. Shulman was satisfied that the paper on the floor at his office was correct. His report suggested that the transactions were authorized and that the paper contained his signature, he said. That report was sent to the bank.

The next day, the bank sent its own report. The report said in part that the documents had "not been in his office from the start."
