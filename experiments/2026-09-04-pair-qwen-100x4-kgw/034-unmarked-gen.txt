I walked to the end of the row, where I sat on the pew in the old-fashioned way: legs crossed, head bowed. The only person there was a woman who sat alone by herself at the back of the room. She stared ahead with her hands clasped together before her.
She never spoke but sometimes her voice seemed close enough to hear, and when she did speak it would be about God or some religious idea that came up as we sang or talked about things. I knew her name was Sister Mary Agnes; I hadn’t heard from her for months. Maybe she’d died, I thought.
