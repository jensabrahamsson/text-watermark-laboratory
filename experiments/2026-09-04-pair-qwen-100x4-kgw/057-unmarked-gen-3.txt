He was a farmer in the hills above San Francisco. He had a son who had been taken by Indian raiders to be killed on the trail back from hunting. It was an old family custom that the eldest son had to kill someone, but he refused.
When his mother died, he sold her house at auction and moved his wife and young son to a tiny cabin near town. But his father had not passed away yet; he was in bad health and needed a son who would take over as his caretaker when he died. The elder son had no children of his own, so he could do what he pleased with the money.
